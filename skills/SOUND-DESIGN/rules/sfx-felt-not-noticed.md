# Felt, not noticed — the level and masking rules for the aesthetic layer

## What it is
The quality standard for the third style of sound effect. Diegetic effects should be *believable*; motion effects should be *synchronised*; aesthetic effects should be **imperceptible as effects and still change how the moment feels**. The test is diagnostic and brutal: if a viewer can point at your sound, it is too loud, too literal, or in the wrong frequency band.

"Imperceptible but effective" is not mysticism, it is masking. Two mechanisms do the work:

- **Simultaneous masking.** A louder sound raises the threshold at which a quieter one becomes audible — the article's worked example is a signal needing to rise from 10 dB SPL to 26 dB SPL to be heard under a masker, i.e. **16 dB** of masking, with another listener showing **20 dB**. Crucially the effect is **frequency-selective**: low-frequency maskers are *"effective over a wide frequency range"*, high-frequency maskers *"only effective over a narrow range"*, and the spread of masking runs **upward** in frequency as the masker gets louder. Voice, which is loudest in the 300–3400 Hz region, therefore masks *upward* into the presence and edge bands very efficiently and hardly masks the sub at all.
- **Temporal masking.** *"Forward (post-) masking: approximately 100 ms"* and *"backward (pre-) masking: approximately 20 ms."* An accent inside the ~100 ms following a loud syllable is substantially hidden; an accent 20 ms *before* one is partly hidden too. An accent in a 600 ms pause is hidden by nothing at all.

Three working rules fall straight out of that:

1. **Put aesthetic accents under speech, not in the pauses.** This is the counter-intuitive one. In a pause, the accent is fully exposed and becomes an event the viewer notices. Under a phrase, partial masking removes its identity while its energy still lands. The exception is silence used deliberately as punctuation, which is a different move.
2. **Choose accents whose energy lives outside the voice's band.** Sub-200 Hz booms and above-8 kHz air are *felt* while being spectrally uncontested; a mid-band swish at the same level competes directly with intelligibility and gets noticed as a rival.
3. **Calibrate by finding the noticing threshold and backing off.** Raise the accent until you can consciously identify it, then drop it 4–6 dB. That lands it in the band this note recommends — **roughly 6–12 dB quieter than motion and diegetic effects**, i.e. **−18 to −24 dB relative to dialogue** rather than the −12 to −15 dB the mix hierarchy gives ordinary effects.

There is also a cognitive-load reading of the same rule. Extraneous load is *"generated by the manner in which information is presented"* and working memory is *"extremely limited in both capacity and duration."* A sound the viewer has to *identify* spends capacity on the presentation; a sound they only feel does not. That is why "felt, not noticed" is a retention rule and not only a taste rule.

## When to use it
- **On every aesthetic-style effect**: cinematic hits under a statement, low tonal beds, textures, camera-move air, body-movement whooshes, the eye-roll swish. The source's own list.
- **On motion effects that should not be the point** — the small text entrances and minor graphic moves. If everything is at −12 dB, everything is foreground; the accents that only need to be *present* belong at this level instead.
- **When a video "sounds designed."** That is the symptom of the aesthetic layer being mixed at motion-layer levels. The fix is 6 dB, not a re-selection.
- **When a viewer asks about a sound.** Real feedback signal: if anyone mentions an individual whoosh, that whoosh failed this standard.
- **Not on diegetic effects that carry information** — a phone ringing that the video is about must be heard.
- **Not on the deliberate punctuation marks** — a smash-cut boom, a riser's arrival, a comedic sting. Those are *meant* to be noticed and are governed by their own notes.

## How to recognise it in a reference video
This is the hardest style to detect by ear, which is exactly the point — so detect it by measurement.

- **Look for transients you cannot hear.** Compare a wide spectrogram against your own listening notes:
  ```bash
  ffmpeg -i ref.wav -lavfi showspectrumpic=s=2048x1024:legend=1 spec.png
  # low-band accents (booms, sub hits) - isolate and count
  ffmpeg -i ref.wav -af "lowpass=f=200,astats=metadata=1:reset=0.1,ametadata=print:key=lavfi.astats.Overall.Peak_level:file=-" -f null -
  # air-band accents
  ffmpeg -i ref.wav -af "highpass=f=8000,astats=metadata=1:reset=0.1,ametadata=print:key=lavfi.astats.Overall.Peak_level:file=-" -f null -
  ```
  Every low- or high-band transient that does **not** correspond to something you consciously heard is a felt-not-noticed accent. Log those; they are the creator's real signature and the thing casual analysis always misses.
- **Measure the per-accent level relative to dialogue.** Take the accent's peak and the nearest dialogue peak. Aesthetic accents land **18–24 dB down**; motion accents **12–15 dB down**. A reference where every accent measures −12 dB has no aesthetic layer, only a motion layer.
- **Check where they sit against speech.** For each accent, note whether it falls under a phrase or inside a pause. A creator working this standard puts most of them **under** speech. A high proportion in pauses means the accents are being used as punctuation, which is a different intent.
- **Check the band.** Accents concentrated below 200 Hz or above 8 kHz are built to be felt. Accents in 600–4000 Hz at low level tend to sound like faults rather than design.
- **The subtraction test, when you have the means.** Even without stems, an A/B of the same moment across a creator's videos (they reuse palettes) reveals which elements are constant and which are per-moment.
- **Count the reverb.** Aesthetic accents in a well-mixed video share the room with the picture — a dry accent under a reverberant scene sticks out and is by definition failing this note ([[sfx-reverb-glue]]).

## Parameters

| Parameter | Default | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aesthetic_gain` | −20 dB → `data-volume="0.1"` | −24 to −18 dB (0.063–0.126) | 6–12 dB under the motion/diegetic level of −12 to −15 dB. |
| `calibration` | notice-threshold minus 5 dB | −4 to −6 dB | Raise until identifiable, then drop. The only reliable per-asset method. |
| `placement` | under speech | under speech · pause (exception only) | Partial masking removes identity, keeps energy. |
| `forward_mask_window` | within 100 ms after a loud syllable | 0–100 ms | Documented post-masking duration. The safest hiding place. |
| `backward_mask_window` | within 20 ms before an onset | 0–20 ms | Documented pre-masking duration. Much narrower. |
| `preferred_bands` | <200 Hz, >8 kHz | — | Uncontested by voice. "Weight" and "Air" in the rack's own band vocabulary. |
| `avoid_band` | 600–4000 Hz | — | "Middle" and "Presence" — where intelligibility lives. |
| `highpass (air accents)` | 4 kHz | 3–8 kHz | Turns a mid-heavy swish into an air accent that can sit louder without competing. |
| `lowpass (weight accents)` | 300 Hz | 200–800 Hz | Turns a full-range boom into pure felt weight. |
| `reverb_wet` | 0.15 | 0.08–0.30 | Room glue. Dry accents are noticed regardless of level. |
| `density` | ≤3 aesthetic accents per minute | 1–5 / min | They are cheap to add and cumulative; the ceiling is in [[sfx-density-fatigue-audit]]. |
| `programme_check` | −14 LUFS I, −1.5 dBTP | — | Whole-mix check after the layer is in. Aesthetic accents should barely move integrated loudness — if they do, they are too loud. |

## Reproduction prompt

```
Mix the aesthetic-layer accent at {{EVENT}} (composition seconds) so it is
felt and not noticed.

1. CONFIRM THE STYLE. This procedure is for aesthetic effects only: textures,
   low tonal beds, camera-move air, body-movement whooshes, eye-roll swishes,
   under-statement hits. If the sound carries information (a phone, a gunshot)
   or must land as punctuation (a smash-cut boom, a riser arrival), stop and
   use the note for that style instead.
2. PLACE IT UNDER SPEECH. Prefer an {{EVENT}} that falls inside a spoken
   phrase, or within 100 ms after a stressed syllable - forward masking of
   about 100 ms will hide its identity while its energy still lands. Only put
   it in a pause if the pause itself is the point.
3. FILTER IT OUT OF THE VOICE'S WAY:
     weight accents -> lowpass 300 Hz, poles 2
     air accents    -> highpass 4000 Hz, poles 2
   Do this BEFORE touching the fader; a filtered accent can sit 4-6 dB louder
   and still not be noticed.
4. CALIBRATE THE LEVEL. Start at data-volume 0.1 (-20 dB). Play the moment and
   raise in 2 dB steps until you can consciously say "there is a sound effect
   there." Note that level, then drop 5 dB. Expect to land between 0.063 and
   0.126.
5. ADD REVERB, wet 0.15, dry 0.9, size 0.5. Without it the accent reads as
   studio-recorded and gets noticed however quiet it is.
6. DO NOT CARVE, and do not put this clip in the voiceover group - a carve
   group must contain voices only or it silently poisons the next analysis.
   Use data-audio-group="sfx" (or "design").
7. RUN THE THREE-PASS VALIDATION:
     pass 1 - play with picture at normal volume, once. Write down every sound
              you noticed. If this accent is on the list, drop it 4 dB.
     pass 2 - mute this accent only (data-hidden) and play the same moment.
              Something must feel missing. If nothing does, it is too quiet or
              unnecessary - delete it rather than raising it.
     pass 3 - measure integrated loudness of the section with and without the
              accent. A change above about 0.5 LU means it is too loud for
              this layer.
8. RE-CHECK THE PROGRAMME: two-pass loudnorm to I=-14, TP=-1.5.

ACCEPTANCE TEST: a listener who has not seen the timeline plays the section
and reports the moment felt "bigger" / "tenser" / "smoother" but cannot name
a sound. If they name the sound, fail: drop 4 dB or move it under a syllable.
If they report nothing at all AND pass 2 showed no loss, fail the other way:
the accent is doing no work and should be removed.
```

## Execution spec

**HyperFrames — filter first, then level, then room; and use `data-hidden` for the mute test.**

```html
<!-- felt weight under a statement: lowpassed boom, -20 dB, room glue -->
<audio id="des-weight-01" src=".media/audio/sfx/low-boom.wav"
       data-audio-group="design"
       data-start="141.83" data-duration="2.40" data-track-index="18" data-volume="0.1"
       data-fx-chain="{&quot;version&quot;:1,&quot;nodes&quot;:[
         {&quot;type&quot;:&quot;lowpass&quot;,&quot;id&quot;:&quot;n1&quot;,&quot;label&quot;:&quot;Weight Only&quot;,&quot;params&quot;:{&quot;frequency&quot;:300,&quot;q&quot;:0.707,&quot;poles&quot;:&quot;2&quot;}},
         {&quot;type&quot;:&quot;reverb&quot;,&quot;id&quot;:&quot;n2&quot;,&quot;label&quot;:&quot;Room Glue&quot;,&quot;params&quot;:{&quot;size&quot;:0.5,&quot;damping&quot;:0.5,&quot;wet&quot;:0.15,&quot;dry&quot;:0.9}},
         {&quot;type&quot;:&quot;gain&quot;,&quot;id&quot;:&quot;n3&quot;,&quot;label&quot;:&quot;Trim&quot;,&quot;params&quot;:{&quot;gain&quot;:0}}]}"
       data-automation="{&quot;version&quot;:1,&quot;lanes&quot;:[{&quot;target&quot;:&quot;volume&quot;,&quot;points&quot;:[{&quot;t&quot;:0,&quot;v&quot;:1},{&quot;t&quot;:1.8,&quot;v&quot;:1},{&quot;t&quot;:2.4,&quot;v&quot;:0}]}]}"></audio>

<!-- the whole aesthetic layer on one bus, so it can be muted or trimmed as a class -->
<hf-audio-group id="design" data-label="Design layer" data-volume="1"></hf-audio-group>
```

Contract points that decide the result:
- **Chain order is signal order**, and the doctrine is *"Subtract before you add, level after you filter, relationships after level, character and ceiling last."* Filter → reverb → gain is that order. A limiter, if any, goes last.
- **`lowpass` defaults to 8000 Hz and `highpass` to 300 Hz** — both wrong for this job, so write `frequency` explicitly. `poles: 2` = 12 dB/oct, `1` = 6 dB/oct.
- **`gain` is the automatable trim** (−60..12 dB, **AUTO**). Automate `fx.n3.gain`, never the `compressor`/`limiter`/`gate`/`bitcrush` parameters — those are AudioWorklets *"configured wholesale — none of their parameters are automatable at all"*, and `reverb`'s `size`/`damping` regenerate the impulse (only `wet`/`dry` automate). The documented workaround is exactly this: *"automate a `gain` stage around it instead."*
- **A lane on a non-automatable parameter is silently inert**, and a lane whose `nodeId` is typo'd is **pruned on read** — so a missing envelope here fails quietly. Check by rendering, not by reading.
- **`data-hidden` is the pass-2 mute**: it *"hides the element in **both** preview and render, overriding its time window"*, non-destructively. Set it on the `design` bus to mute the whole layer at once — a bus's `data-hidden` *"drops every member from the mix."*
- **The bus is also how the whole layer gets one trim.** *"one chain, one fader, one automation clock for every member"* — and on a bus, automation `t` is **composition** time, not clip-local, which is the one asymmetry to remember.
- **Do not carve, and keep the clip out of the `voiceover` group.** `data-fx-carve` is clip-only and belongs on a music bed.
- **`reverb` adds `chainTailSeconds`**, so the accent rings past its `data-duration` — usually desirable here.
- **No de-esser exists** if an air accent turns sibilant against the voice; the honest fallback is *"a narrow `peaking` cut in the Edge band — sweep 5–9 kHz, Q 3–4, −3 to −5 dB, always-on."*

**ffmpeg — measurement is the only real validator.** *"Almost no static gate covers the mix"*, and *"nothing validates the chain or the effect lanes at all."*
```bash
# integrated loudness, with and without the accent (pass 3)
ffmpeg -i with.wav    -af loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11:print_format=json -f null -
ffmpeg -i without.wav -af loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11:print_format=json -f null -
# momentary trace (400 ms R128 window) to see whether the accent moves the needle
ffmpeg -i with.wav -af "ebur128=metadata=1:framelog=verbose" -f null - 2>&1 | grep ' M:'
# band-limited accent census
ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af "lowpass=f=200,astats=metadata=1:reset=0.1" -f null -
```
And the diagnosis rule this whole note obeys: *"The absolute spectrum of a single unknown voice cannot be diagnosed"* — compare against something **inside the same file**, i.e. the same section with the accent muted.

**Epidemic Sound — pick material that is already in the right band.** The cheapest way to be felt rather than noticed is to fetch a sound that has no mid-band to begin with:
```
# weight: long low booms (the shelf verified by the smash-cut note)
SearchSoundEffects {
  filter: { tagSlugs: { matchType: ALL, values: ["designed--boom"] },
            duration: { min: 1000, max: 3500 } },
  sort: { by: POPULARITY, order: DESCENDING }, first: 20 }

# air on body movement and camera moves
SearchSoundEffects {
  filter: { tagSlugs: { matchType: ANY, values: ["swooshes--whoosh","swooshes--swish"] },
            duration: { min: 300, max: 1200 } },
  sort: { by: POPULARITY, order: DESCENDING }, first: 20 }
# verified 2026-08-28: "Swooshes, Whoosh, Designed, Generic, Air" (0.55 s),
#                     "Swooshes, Whoosh, Mids" (1.75 s), "Swooshes, Whoosh, Wind" (1.64 s)
```
Read the titles: *"Air"* and *"Wind"* are already high-band and need almost no filtering; *"Mids"* is the one that will fight the voice. Then `SearchSimilarToSoundEffect { id }` to build the palette, and `DownloadSoundEffect` into `.media/audio/sfx/`.

**Remotion:** the same accent as an `<Audio volume={0.1}>` with a filtered source. Concept only; no Remotion runtime in this project.

## Failure modes
- **Aesthetic accents mixed at motion-layer level.** Everything is foreground, the video "sounds designed", and the viewer starts listening to the edit instead of the content. Fix: −18 to −24 dB, and use the notice-threshold-minus-5 calibration per asset.
- **Accents parked in the pauses.** Fully exposed, so they become events. Fix: move them under a phrase, or inside the ~100 ms after a stressed syllable.
- **Mid-band accents.** They compete with intelligibility, then get quieted until they do nothing at all. Fix: filter to weight (<300 Hz) or air (>4 kHz) and re-level.
- **Dry accents.** Noticed at any level, because they are the only element with no room. Fix: reverb wet 0.08–0.30.
- **Raising an accent that "does nothing".** If pass 2 shows nothing missing when it is muted, the accent is unnecessary; raising it makes it noticeable *and* unnecessary. Fix: delete it.
- **Automating a compressor or limiter parameter to "duck" the accent.** Silently inert — those nodes have zero automatable parameters. Fix: automate a `gain` stage around them.
- **Cumulative creep.** Twenty inaudible accents still add up in the integrated loudness and in fatigue. Fix: ≤3 per minute, and check integrated loudness with the layer bypassed via the bus's `data-hidden`.
- **Known gap:** the −18 to −24 dB band and the notice-threshold-minus-5 calibration are derived from the masking figures above plus this library's own level hierarchy; no published study fixes a "subliminal SFX" level. The masking numbers (16–20 dB threshold elevation, 100 ms forward, 20 ms backward, upward spread) are the defensible part, and the calibration procedure is what makes the number reproducible on any given asset.
