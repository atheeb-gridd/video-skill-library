# Pick the timing model — word, phrase or hybrid — before a single cue is cut

## What it is

Every caption track is generated by one of three functions over the same word-level transcript, and the choice determines everything downstream: how many cues exist, what the reading-speed maths means, whether cut boundaries matter, and which motion is even possible.

- **Word-level.** One cue per word, or per one-to-three-word group, chained so the box never clears. `cue.start` is the word's onset, `cue.end` is the next word's onset. Reading rate is not a design parameter — the track inherits the speaker's rate by construction. This is the short-form register.
- **Phrase-level.** One cue per clause or sentence fragment, up to two lines, held while the speaker finishes it. `cue.start` is the first word's onset, `cue.end` is the last word's offset plus a hold. Reading rate becomes the binding constraint, and the viewer can read ahead. This is the broadcast register, and the one every published standard was written for.
- **Hybrid.** A phrase-level cue with a word-level highlight advancing inside it. Two independent timing streams over the same transcript: the card obeys phrase timing, the highlight obeys word onsets. It is the only model that gives read-ahead *and* an exact "where is the voice now" signal, and it is the most expensive to author.

The choice is not aesthetic. Word-level throws away read-ahead, which is what carries comprehension when the viewer's attention leaves the caption to look at the picture. Phrase-level throws away the per-word sync signal, which is what makes a muted feed video watchable at a glance. The [PLOS study on subtitle speed](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0199331) found that at 12 cps viewers re-read roughly two of every three subtitles, versus about one in five at faster speeds — read-ahead time that is not needed gets spent on re-reading, which is why an over-held phrase track feels worse, not better.

## When to use it

- **Word-level** when the deliverable is vertical short-form, sound-on, the speaker is under ~200 wpm, and the caption is the primary visual rhythm. Also when the caption must survive being read in a 1.5-second scroll-past.
- **Phrase-level** when the deliverable is a long-form horizontal video, an accessibility track, or anything a viewer might read while also reading on-screen graphics. Anything that ships a sidecar file is phrase-level by default — SRT and VTT players are built for it.
- **Hybrid** when the speech is fast, the content is dense (numbers, terms, names), or the profile shows a held card with one word changing colour. It is the most common signature in the modern short-form set.
- **Do not mix models inside one video** except at a deliberate structural boundary — a full-frame topic card is not a model change, it is a different element. Two caption models alternating reads as a bug.
- Decide **after** the role decision in [[sub-caption-role-decision]] and **before** segmentation in [[sub-cue-segmentation-three-word]].

## How to recognise it in a reference video

- **Cue-change rate.** Count text changes per 10 seconds at native frame rate. Word-level runs **18–40 changes / 10 s**; phrase-level runs **3–7**; hybrid shows two rates at once — a card rate of 3–7 and a highlight rate of 18–40.
- **Words visible per frame.** Sample 20 frames mid-cue. Word-level: mode 1–3, never above 3. Phrase-level: 5–14 across up to two lines. Hybrid: 3–7 on one line with exactly one word differing in colour, weight or scale.
- **Read-ahead test.** Freeze on the frame where a word is first spoken. If the words *after* it are already on screen, the model is phrase or hybrid. If the frame shows only the word being spoken, it is word-level.
- **Box persistence.** Step frame by frame across a cue boundary mid-sentence. Word-level chained tracks never blank the plate; phrase tracks blank it for 2–6 frames between cues.
- **Reading rate.** Sum on-screen characters ÷ seconds. Phrase tracks land at **12–20 CPS**; word-level chained tracks routinely hit **25–35 CPS** and are legitimate only because the audio carries the meaning.
- **Line count.** Two lines anywhere in the track rules out pure word-level.
- **Position stability.** Word-level tracks usually centre-anchor and grow both ways; phrase tracks left-align or bottom-anchor with a fixed baseline. Measure the baseline y as a percentage of frame height across 20 samples — a variance above ~1 % of frame height means the cue box is being re-laid-out per cue.

## Parameters

| Parameter | Default | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `timing_model` | hybrid | word / phrase / hybrid | One per video. Recorded in `PROFILE.md`, not decided per scene. |
| `words_per_cue_word_level` | 2.4 mean | 1–3 | Above 3 it is no longer word-level. |
| `words_per_cue_phrase_level` | 8 | 5–14 | Up to 42 characters per line, two lines (Netflix). |
| `cue_change_rate_word` | 26 / 10 s | 18–40 | Direct measurement from a reference. |
| `cue_change_rate_phrase` | 5 / 10 s | 3–7 | Below 3 the track is stalling. |
| `read_ahead_window` | 0 s (word) / 0.8 s (phrase) | 0–1.5 s | How much unspoken text is visible. The whole argument for phrase cues. |
| `reading_rate_target` | 17 CPS | 12–20 CPS | Binding for phrase and hybrid. Advisory only for chained word-level, sound-on. |
| `speech_rate_switch_point` | 200 wpm | 180–220 wpm | Above this, word-level cards swap faster than they can be read; move to hybrid. |
| `lines_max` | 2 | 1–2 | Word-level and hybrid should be 1. |
| `model_changes_per_video` | 0 | 0 | Any value above 0 needs a written justification. |
| `sidecar_required` | false | true/false | If true, the model must be phrase-level — see [[sub-sidecar-timing-fidelity]]. |

## Reproduction prompt

```
Choose and record the caption timing model for {{PROJECT}} before generating
any cues.

INPUTS: the word-level transcript ({text,start,end} in seconds), the target
platform {{PLATFORM}}, the deliverable aspect {{ASPECT}}, whether a sidecar
caption file is required ({{SIDECAR}}), and the measured speech rate
{{WPM}} computed as (word count / duration) * 60 over the whole programme.

DECIDE, in this order:
1. If {{SIDECAR}} is true, or {{ASPECT}} is horizontal long-form, choose
   PHRASE. Sidecar players cannot express a chained word track.
2. Else if {{WPM}} > 200, choose HYBRID - chained word cards swap faster than
   they can be fixated at that rate.
3. Else if the profile's measured cue-change rate is 18-40 per 10 s and no
   sampled frame shows more than 3 words, choose WORD.
4. Else choose HYBRID.

Then write into design-subtitles.md: the model name, words per cue, the
target reading rate in CPS, the line count, and one sentence naming what the
model is buying (read-ahead, or per-word sync, or both).

ACCEPTANCE TEST: exactly one model is recorded for the whole video; the
recorded words-per-cue and cue-change rate are inside the ranges for that
model; if PHRASE, no cue exceeds two lines of 42 characters and no cue
exceeds {{CPS_CAP}}=17 CPS; if WORD, no cue exceeds 3 words; if HYBRID, every
card carries exactly one highlighted word at any sampled frame.
```

## Execution spec

HyperFrames has **no caption primitive** — no `data-caption`, no SRT/VTT ingest, no subtitle renderer. A caption track is an ordinary sub-composition whose GSAP timeline writes text and animates a box, and the reference implementation is `compositions/captions.html`. That file hard-codes **phrase-level with a fixed 5-words-per-line `for` loop**, `start` = first word's start, `end` = last word's end. Whichever model you choose, you are replacing that loop.

The model determines the DOM shape, and getting this wrong is expensive to undo:

- **Phrase-level** can use the reference's single reused `#caption-box` + `#caption-text` and its four-tween cycle per line (`set` visible → `to` opacity 1 with the text swap in `onStart` → `to` opacity 0 at `line.end` → `set` hidden at `line.end + 0.1`). Cheap DOM, but the `onStart` text swap is not seek-robust backwards.
- **Word-level chained** needs one element per cue with its own opacity envelope and `tl.set()` handovers, because the reference cycle fades out and back in between every cue.
- **Hybrid** needs one element per card plus one `<span>` per word inside it, so the highlight can be driven independently of the card.

All authored time is **seconds**; there is no frame attribute. Convert at authoring time and leave the frame count as a comment. Cue arrays are **inlined into the composition** — nothing reads `transcript.json` at render time. Word timings come from `npx hyperframes transcribe <file>` (Parakeet default, whisper.cpp fallback on non-Apple-silicon hosts).

Track index convention: captions live on `1+` with audio at `10+`; layering is CSS `z-index`, not `data-track-index`, which is display only.

## Failure modes
- **Choosing the model implicitly by copying a template.** The reference `captions.html` 5-word loop is a phrase-level decision made by a `for` loop, and it silently ships as the house model. Correction: record the decision before authoring.
- **Word-level for a muted-first deliverable.** A 30 CPS chained track is legible sound-on and unreadable muted, because there is no read-ahead to spend. Correction: hybrid, with the card held at 17 CPS.
- **Phrase-level under fast delivery.** At 220 wpm phrase cues either exceed the CPS cap or lag the voice by a full clause. Correction: hybrid.
- **Mixing models across scenes.** Reads as two different videos edited together. Correction: one model, with full-frame topic cards as a separate element.
- **Deciding the model after the DOM exists.** Converting a single-box phrase track into a hybrid means rebuilding every cue. Correction: decide first.
- **Treating the highlight as a style choice.** In hybrid the highlight is a *timing stream*, and it needs word onsets accurate to ~2 frames. Without forced alignment it will drift — see [[sub-forced-alignment-word-timings]].
