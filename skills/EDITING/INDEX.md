# EDITING — index

The **81 notes** in `skills/EDITING/rules/` describe how a video is *cut*: where the join goes, how long a shot is held, what order the material arrives in, and what keeps a viewer past the point where they would otherwise leave. This library owns the timeline — motion, sound and captions all attach to decisions made here, which is why it is designed and read first.

**How it is organised.** Every note carries a `type/` (what kind of move it is) and a `family/` (the small group of notes that solve one problem together). `skills/EDITING/SKILL.md` routes by tag query; this page routes by browsing, so a note with a wrong or missing tag is still reachable. If you know the shape of the problem, scan the families below; if you already know the vocabulary, the tag queries in the router are faster.

**By `type/`** — `cut` 24 · `pacing` 18 · `structure` 18 · `retention` 13 · `transition` 7 · `motion` 1  
**By `difficulty/`** — low 32 · medium 37 · high 12  
**Families** — 42

The `type/` values do different jobs. `cut` and `transition` are the joins themselves. `pacing` governs how long things are held and how often they change. `structure` is the order of the whole video, and `retention` is the subset of structural moves aimed squarely at keeping someone watching. The single `motion` note here is the finishing pass, which belongs to the edit rather than to the animation.

## Start here

Eight notes, in this order, before picking techniques off the shelf.

1. **[[cut-invisible-storytelling-doctrine]]** — The governing principle. Every cut needs a job, and the best ones are never noticed — read this before any technique note.
2. **[[pace-subtractive-first-pass]]** — The first thing you actually do to a timeline: four removals, coarse to fine, before any visual work.
3. **[[cut-straight-hard-cut]]** — The default cut. Every other note here is a deviation from it, so know what the baseline costs.
4. **[[struct-stimulation-budget]]** — The most-linked note in the vault. It sets how much cutting, motion and sound the format can carry at all.
5. **[[pace-cut-density-from-viewer-intent]]** — Turns "fast" or "slow" into a defensible number derived from what the audience came for.
6. **[[cut-j-audio-leads-picture]]** — The highest-leverage cut once the basics are in place, and the entry point to the whole `audio-led` family.
7. **[[pace-cut-on-the-beat]]** — Where the timeline meets the music bed — the busiest cross-skill hinge in the vault.
8. **[[struct-name-define-demonstrate]]** — The teaching module the reference creators reuse constantly; the shape most explainer segments should take.
